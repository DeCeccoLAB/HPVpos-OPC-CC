# Transcriptomic clustering identifies shared molecular programs across Human Papillomavirus-driven oropharyngeal and cervical carcinomas 
Elena Colombo†*, Armando G. Licata†, Bahareh Gharehkhanisenejani†, Stefano Cavalieri, Federico Rossignoli, Deborah Lenoci, Federico Pistore, Daniela Alterio, Domenica Lorussoe, Luca Boldrini, Giorgio Boganic, Umberto Leone Roberti Maggiore, Valentina Chiappa, Francesco Raspagliesi, Silvana Canevari, Lisa Licitraa, Marta Lucchetta*, Loris De Cecco* 

Human Papillomavirus (HPV) drives carcinogenesis in distinct anatomical sites, including the oropharynx and cervix, yet the molecular convergence between these tumors remains poorly defined. We integrated transcriptomic data from 553 HPV-positive oropharyngeal carcinomas (OPC) and 488 cervical carcinomas (CC), including a newly profiled institutional cohort, to characterize shared transcriptional programs. Unsupervised consensus clustering identified three gene-expression clusters in each tumor type. Subclass mapping showed significant correspondence between OPC2–CC1 (p=0.008) and OPC3–CC2 (p=0.001), whereas OPC1 and CC3 did not correspond. The two correspondences coincided with shared inflammatory (TNFα–NFκB) and immune/interferon programs, respectively. Exploratory in silico estimates (xCell, Radiosensitivity Index, Immunophenoscore) differed among clusters, mostly with small effect sizes, and were not validated against functional or treatment-response data. In OPC, the global cluster term remained associated with both overall survival and disease-free survival after adjustment for age, sex and stage, with the strongest and most consistent adverse association observed for OPC2; the adjusted OPC3 effect was significant for DFS but not for OS. CC survival data were limited and exploratory. These findings indicate partial, cluster-specific transcriptional convergence between HPV-driven OPC and CC, rather than tissue-independent molecular subtypes.
